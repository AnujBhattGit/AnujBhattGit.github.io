PROJECT / 01

Electric Guitar Amplifier

As a guitar player, the circuitry behind electric guitars and their amplifiers is often abstracted away. This project was about opening that box: investigating the different classes of analog amplifiers, how they work together on a single board, and how a pluck on a string ends up as a full, shaped sound at the speaker.

DESIGN & CALCULATIONS

The amplifier is built around a two-stage design: a preamp stage that sets the input gain and shapes tone, followed by a power stage that drives the speaker load. I chose component values by working backward from a target gain and output swing, then checked each stage's frequency response so the guitar's full range came through without unwanted rolloff.

The full derivation — including bias point calculations, gain equations for each stage, and component tolerances — is in the write-up linked below. The schematic and the key results are summarized here.

BUILD LOG

How it came together.

WEEK 1

Choosing a topology

Started by comparing amplifier classes to decide what fit a guitar signal's dynamic range. Landed on a two-stage design as a balance between simplicity and having independent control over gain and tone shaping.

WEEK 2

First bring-up

Built the preamp stage first and measured gain against my hand calculations. Initial gain was off from the target — traced it to underestimating the op-amp's output impedance loading the next stage, and adjusted the feedback resistor to compensate.

WEEK 3

Adding the power stage

Connected the power stage and drove a test speaker load. Saw clipping at lower input levels than expected, which pointed to the power stage's supply rail being too close to the required output swing — increased headroom by revisiting the supply design.

WEEK 4

Noise cleanup

Noticed hum on the scope at idle. Isolated it to a ground loop between the preamp and power stage grounds and fixed it with a single-point ground reference.
